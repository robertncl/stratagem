Overview

Stratagem is a turn-based strategy game of Western Europe, from 1400 AD to the Space Age. You lead one of eight great powers. Explore the continent, found cities, research 51 technologies over 11 eras, and outlast, outbuild or outfly your rivals: seven computer-led powers and 29 independent states.

  • 325 turns, 2 years each: 1400 to 2050 AD.
  • About 4,600 hexes of real geography, each about 40 km across, from Ireland to the Balkans and Tunis.
  • Four ways to win: conquest, domination, the space race, or the highest score at the end.

Your first turns

You start in your historical capital (size 2, borders already two tiles out) with two Levies, a Scout and a Settler.

  1. Choose research (T). Pottery, Bronze Working or your realm's favourite are good first picks.
  2. Choose production in the capital: click it twice to open it. A second Settler or a Scout is a strong start.
  3. Send the Scout exploring: select it and right-click far away.
  4. Fortify a Levy in the capital (F), so it stops asking for orders.
  5. Walk the Settler to a good site, with food, a river or coast, three or more tiles from any city, and press B.
  6. End the turn (Enter). The button in the bottom-right always names the next thing that needs you.

AI powers never declare war in the first 12 turns, so you have time to expand.

Realms & leaders

Each great power starts at its historical capital, with its own trait and a free technology. It is led by a representative figure of its history, whose portrait you'll see on the selection screen and in diplomacy.

Independent states

Independent states can't found new cities, build wonders or train Settlers. They defend their capital, grow, build up, and will make peace with you after a few turns of war. Capture their cities to grow your realm; many are walled. The Papal States hold Rome and the Hafsids hold Tunis, the holy cities of Catholicism and Islam.

    The land

    The map is generated from real geography: coastlines, the Alps, Pyrenees and Apennines, 26 rivers, forests and marshes. Each city works the tiles around it, so the land decides how a city grows.

    • Rivers add +1 gold to a tile, and moving from one river tile to another costs only 1, so they act as roads.
    • Scouts pay 1 movement for any land (2 for mountains).
    • Mounted units get no terrain defence bonus outside cities.
    • The sea. With Sailing, land units can embark onto coastal waters and lakes, and with Navigation, onto the ocean. Embarking or landing uses a whole turn's movement. Embarked units sail on a cog, can't attack, and defend very weakly, so escort them or keep them away from enemies.

    Cities

    Founding

    A Settler founds a city with B. The site must be land other than mountains, not inside another realm's borders, and at least 3 tiles from every city. New cities take the name of the nearest real town. A Settler can only be finished in a city of size 2 or more, and costs that city 1 population. Your first city is your capital, which makes +1 food, +1 production, +3 gold, +3 science and +2 culture.

    Citizens, tiles and focus

    Each citizen works one tile within 2 tiles of the city, on land the city has claimed and that no enemy stands on. The city centre is worked for free (at least 2 food, 2 production and 1 gold). Cities choose their own tiles, guided by a focus you set in the city panel: Balanced, Food, Production or Gold. Whatever the focus, a city gives up other yields for food rather than starve. Opening a city outlines its worked tiles on the map and shows each tile's yields. A city with more citizens than tiles has idle citizens: it has outgrown its land.

    Growth

    Each citizen eats 2 food. The surplus is stored, and when the store is full the city grows by one. A Granary keeps a quarter of the store after growing. If the store would go below zero, the city shrinks instead.

    Borders and culture

    Culture accumulates in each city. At 12 culture its borders grow from 1 to 2 tiles, and at 90 to 3, claiming every unowned tile in range. Temples, wonders, your capital, some faiths and some realm traits add culture.

    Production

    • A city builds one thing at a time: a unit, a building, a wonder or project, or Wealth, which turns half its production into gold.
    • Switching between a unit and a building loses half the production stored so far.
    • When something finishes, up to a turn's worth of extra production (at least 30) carries over to the next item.
    • Buying finishes the current item next turn for 2.2 gold per missing production point, plus 10. You can't buy wonders or national projects, or buy in a city in unrest.
    • If a rival finishes a wonder you were building, the city stops but keeps its production for the next item.

    Capture and unrest

    A captured city loses one population, its stored food and production, and each ordinary building with a 40% chance (wonders always survive). It then spends 3 turns in unrest, making no production, gold or science, or 2 turns more if its faith differs from its new owner's. Retaking your original capital makes it your capital again.

    Gold & upkeep

    • Income: gold from worked tiles (rivers, wine, silver…), the city centre, buildings like Markets and Banks, faith and traits.
    • Building upkeep is listed with each building. Wonders and projects cost nothing to keep.
    • Unit upkeep: 3 units plus one per city are free, and each unit beyond that costs 1 gold a turn.
    • Bankruptcy: if your treasury would fall below zero, your cheapest military unit deserts each turn until it doesn't.
    • The top bar shows your treasury and its net change per turn.

    Research

    Every city produces science: 1, plus 0.6 per citizen, plus buildings, wonders and capital bonuses. Your science goes into one technology at a time. Leftover science carries into the next one, and science made while nothing is chosen is banked.

    • Cost is set by era (shown below), and goes up 4% for every city you own beyond your fourth.
    • Research goals. On the research screen, click a locked tech to make it your goal. Each time research finishes, the cheapest tech on the way is picked for you. The screen numbers the path, and the top bar shows the goal.
    • Hover a tech to light up everything it still needs (cyan) and what it leads to (dashed).
    • Newer units replace older ones in the build list. For example, once you can build Pikemen, Spearmen are no longer offered.
    • Special effects: Sailing and Navigation let units take to the sea; the first great power to learn Printing Press starts the Reformation; Space Flight opens the space race; Satellites chart the whole map.

    In computer-only games the medieval eras take about 140 turns and the Space Age arrives around turn 280–300. Below is the whole tree, era by era. ★ marks a world wonder and ◆ a national project.

    Units

    29 units in six lines: infantry, mounted, ranged, siege, recon and civilians, plus super weapons. Each line is replaced by its successor as you research, from the Levy's pitchfork to Mechanized Infantry and from the Horseman to Modern Armor. Units have 100 HP, heal when they don't act, and become veterans (★, +25% strength) after 4 fights or when trained in a city with Barracks.

    Combat

    Hover an enemy with a unit selected to see the forecast: both strengths and the damage each side should take. Damage grows with the ratio of the two strengths, and both sides lose some strength as they lose HP (a unit at 0 HP fights at half strength).

    Strength

    • Attacking: attack (or ranged) strength, +25% for veterans, plus realm traits (Castile, the Kalmar Union), the Pikeman/Spearman bonus against mounted units, and the siege bonus against cities.
    • Defending: defence strength, plus the terrain bonus (none for mounted units outside cities), +25% when fortified or in a city, and the city's own bonus: 15% plus 2.5% per citizen (up to 25%), with Walls +60%, a Castle another +60% and the Alhambra +100%.
    • An embarked unit defends at strength 2, whatever its type.

    Ranged and siege

    Archers, Crossbowmen, Gatling and Machine Guns and all siege engines strike from 2 tiles away (Rocket Artillery from 3) and take no retaliation, though ranged attacks do 15% less damage. They can't fire from the sea, and they can't capture cities. Siege engines are +100% against units in cities.

    Taking a city

    Kill every defender, then march a melee or mounted unit in. Enemy civilians on a tile you enter are captured (Settlers) or scattered.

    Healing

    A unit that didn't move or fight heals at the end of the turn: 20 HP in your own city, 15 in your territory, 10 in neutral land and 5 in enemy land. Fortified units outside cities heal 5 more, and units next to an enemy army heal half as fast.

    Buildings

    27 buildings, from the Granary to the Spaceport. Some need another building first: Castle needs Walls, University needs a Library, and so on.

    Wonders & projects

    Only one of each world wonder exists in the world. If a rival finishes first, your city keeps its production for something else. Wonders can't be bought, survive capture, and add 20 to the score of whoever holds them.

    National projects are built once per realm and belong to the realm, not the city. They can't be bought.

    Faith

    Every city follows a faith, shown by the badge on its name label.

    • State religion. Your realm's faith is your capital's. A city of another faith is in dissent and loses 20% of its production. If your capital converts, your state religion changes with it, and your other cities may then be in dissent.
    • Pressure. Each turn, every city presses its faith on every city within 6 tiles (at half strength beyond 3), and twice as hard on itself. A city presses with 1, plus 1 for every 4 citizens, plus its buildings (Temple +2, Cluny Abbey +2, Notre-Dame +3, El Escorial +2), plus 3 for a holy city. Pressure builds up in each city, and when another faith's total outweighs the city's own by a fifth (plus a little), the city converts. The city panel shows how its people are split.
    • Holy cities make +3 gold and +2 culture, press harder, and never convert.
    • The Reformation. The first great power to learn Printing Press founds Protestantism in its capital, which becomes the faith's holy city. That realm's other cities stay Catholic until it converts them.
    • Missionaries (Theology, 40 production) carry the faith of the city that trains them. Next to or inside a city of another faith, press P to convert it at once, which uses up the missionary. They can't preach in holy cities or in cities of realms you're at war with.
    • AIs send missionaries mostly to win back their own dissenting cities, and are readier to make war on realms of another faith.

    Diplomacy

    • Every realm starts at peace with every other. Declare war from the Diplomacy screen, or by attacking (you're asked to confirm).
    • A peace treaty lasts at least 10 turns, and neither side can declare war until it ends.
    • You can propose peace to a realm you're at war with. A great power that has been at war with you for at least 5 turns accepts if its army is weaker than yours, or the war has dragged on past 30 turns; between 10 and 30 turns it sometimes accepts anyway. Independent states accept after 4 turns.
    • AIs offer peace too: you'll be asked at the start of your turn.
    • When choosing whom to attack, AIs look for weaker neighbours, and lean toward realms of another faith and realms that have used the bomb. On Hard, they lean toward you.

    Super weapons

    With Nuclear Fission, build the Manhattan Project to develop the bomb. After that, any of your cities can build:

    • Atomic Bombs: range 10, blast radius 1 (7 tiles).
    • Nuclear Missiles (also needs Rocketry): range 24, blast radius 2 (19 tiles).

    Launching

    A weapon waits in the city that built it. Select it, press L (or ☢ Launch), and hover a target. The blast is previewed in red (grey where you can't launch), and the tooltip lists the units destroyed, the cities devastated, any new wars and any Missile Shield. Click and confirm to launch.

    Effects

    • Every unit in the blast is destroyed, yours included.
    • Cities keep 40% of their people at ground zero, 60% one tile out and 80% two out. They lose many buildings (wonders survive), all stored food and production, and fall into unrest.
    • Land in the blast is left in fallout and yields nothing for 20 turns. The map marks it with a trefoil.
    • Striking a realm you're at peace with means war, and you can't launch during a peace treaty. Every AI is readier to make war on a realm that has used the bomb.

    Defence

    A Missile Shield (Satellites) shoots down 75% of strikes aimed within 3 tiles of its city. Super weapons never fight, move or defend, and are lost if their city falls.

    How the AI uses them

    An AI develops the bomb once a rival has it or it's at war with a great power, and keeps up to two weapons. It launches at most once every 5 turns, and only at great powers it has been fighting for a while. It never strikes where the blast would hit its own cities or drag in another great power, and it strikes more readily when it's losing. Build Missile Shields in your key cities once your rivals have the bomb.

    The space race

    1. Research Space Flight and build the Apollo Program (a national project).
    2. Build a Spaceport (needs a Factory) in one or more productive cities.
    3. In your Spaceport cities, build the colony ship's three parts: the Launch Booster (Space Flight), the Orbital Habitat (Robotics) and the Fusion Drive (Fusion). Different cities can build different parts at the same time.
    4. The ship launches when the last part is done, and lands 10 turns later. The first ship to land wins.

    If your capital falls while the ship is in flight, the ship is lost and its parts must be built again. Rivals know this, so guard your capital during the flight, and watch for enemy launches in the log.

    Victory & score

    Conquest
    Be the last great power standing.
    Domination
    Hold the original capital of every great power at once.
    Space
    Be the first to land a colony ship on a new world.
    Score
    Have the highest score when turn 325 ends (2050 AD).

    Score = 8 per city + 3 per citizen + 5 per tech + 20 per world wonder + 1 per 4 tiles of territory. The Diplomacy screen shows every realm's score.

    You're eliminated, and lose, when your last city falls. Units alone don't keep you in the game.

    Strategy tips

    • Expand early. Cities are your engine. Train Settlers while land is free, and before rivals take the best river and coastal sites.
    • Mind the research penalty. Each city beyond the fourth makes research 4% dearer, but a good city still pays for itself many times over.
    • Use focus. Switch a city to Food to grow it quickly, then to Production to build a wonder. Rivers and wine or silver make gold-focus cities.
    • Walls before war. Walls and a Castle more than double a city's defence. Defenders fortified in a walled city on a hill are very hard to dislodge.
    • Bring siege. Catapults through to Rocket Artillery double against cities and take no retaliation. Soften the defenders, then send in melee.
    • Keep the faith. Before you conquer a city of another faith, have a Missionary ready to convert it and end its dissent.
    • Aim for a goal. Set a research goal (for example, Theology for Missionaries and Notre-Dame, or Gunpowder for Musketeers) and let the steps pick themselves.
    • Shields up. Once a rival has the bomb (☢ in Diplomacy), build Missile Shields in your capital and your biggest cities.
    • Race smart. Build Spaceports in your two best production cities so the ship's parts go up side by side, and keep a strong garrison in the capital until the ship lands.

    Saves & settings

    • The game autosaves every time you end a turn. The menu (Esc) offers Continue and three manual save slots.
    • Saves live in your browser's local storage (keys starting with stratagem:), so they stay with that browser and that site.
    • Games saved by older versions load and are brought up to date: they gain faiths and run on to 2050.
    • The game runs entirely in your browser. To run it yourself, see the project's README (npm install, then npm run dev).
